# Synthetic delivery-risk fixture

`scenario.json` fixes the seed, weekly cohorts, chronological cutoffs and alert budget before evaluation. `risk_model/fixtures.py` materializes all inputs into each run's ignored `artifacts/` directory. There are 624 invented order lines, two anonymous customer/project scopes, two shared production lines and 1,248 order change events. This is a bounded correctness exercise, not a production throughput benchmark.

The existing incremental ETL validates and publishes every order change. Separate planning and shared-line snapshots add delivery dates, allocation, pickup forecasts, prior yield and committed capacity. Their availability ledger belongs to this offline replay; it does not change the ETL order contract. Three day-level timestamps are retained: business effective date, source observation date and simulated publication/availability date. These are invented historical dates, not evidence of actual past ingestion. A production implementation must obtain them from source CDC and publication records.

Each line's committed units include both fixture projects and additional reserved work. The same line/window snapshot is shared across customers. Gross capacity and commitments refer to the same upcoming 14-day planning window. Recent yield is a trailing historical measurement ending before prediction. Fixture totals are checked to ensure tracked orders do not exceed total shared-line commitments.

Every order has a v1 known at prediction and a retroactively effective v2 first received three days later. Every plan and capacity snapshot also has a later revision. Historical features must ignore those revisions. Delivery ledgers contain partial shipments, completion, observation and source-closure dates. Open, cancelled and not-yet-mature outcomes are excluded explicitly.

The simulator samples delays from a fixed probabilistic formula involving load, schedule slack, design changes, allocation, yield and pickup gaps. That formula is visible in source. Training does not receive its probabilities or latent outcome draw. Because outcomes are generated by an assumed process, all measured performance is synthetic and cannot validate real feature importance, actual capacity decisions or business benefit. The fixed baseline intentionally uses only load, slack and yield.

Only one prediction per order is generated. Repeated predictions, cancellations after intervention, returns, changing order-line commitments and time-varying intervention policies need separate production contracts. No real customer, employer, mailbox or engineering data is used.
